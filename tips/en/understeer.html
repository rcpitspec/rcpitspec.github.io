How to Fix RC Car Understeer (Pushing Wide)

Symptoms

  • Car won't turn in when you initiate the corner
  • Front pushes wide mid-corner or on throttle exit
  • Struggling to rotate around tight hairpin corners

5 Quick Adjustments to Try First

  1. Increase Steering Dual Rate … Check your transmitter first if you previously reduced steering travel
  2. Reduce Rear Toe-In … Too much rear toe-in locks the rear end and prevents rotation
  3. Soften the Rear Differential … A softer gear diff allows the rear end to rotate more freely
  4. Lay Down Front Shocks … Laying shocks down generally increases front-end grip and turn-in
  5. Soften or Remove the Front Sway Bar … A stiff front bar can cause entry push on lower grip tracks

※ These are general guidelines.
Setup effects can vary depending on chassis and surface grip. It's best to change one setting at a time.

In-Depth Setup Guide

When It Pushes vs What to Check

PhaseMost Likely Areas to Check
Corner entry (turn-in push)Steering rate, front sway bar, front shock angle
Mid-corner to exit pushRear toe-in, rear diff oil, excessive rear traction
Only tight hairpinsFront spool (solid axle) vs gear differential
First few laps onlyTires or differential oils not up to operating temperature
Late in hot runsFront tires overheating or falling off

Steering Dual Rate

If you dialed down your steering rate earlier to calm down a loose rear end, you might simply lack steering angle now.
Before wrenching on suspension parts, try bumping your dual rate up 5% to 10% on your transmitter.

Rear Toe-In & Camber

Reducing rear toe-in frees up the car to rotate into corners and improves straight-line speed.
Adding a small amount of rear camber can also help free up the rear tires if they have too much forward bite.

Differential Tuning

A softer rear gear differential lets the rear tires differentiate easier, helping the chassis pivot around the corner apex.
Switching from a tight spool or stiff diff to a properly weighted gear diff often unlocks much better rotation.

Shocks & Sway Bars

Laying down the front shocks or slightly softening the rear springs helps shift balance forward during cornering.
If installing a front sway bar caused the car to push, try disconnecting it or switching to a thinner wire.

Chassis Stiffness

Fully tightening every top deck brace and stiffener screw can make the chassis too rigid, causing low-speed push.
Removing center brace screws allows the chassis to flex, which can restore needed mechanical grip.

Watch Out for Over-Adjusting

Adding too much steering traction can cause snap oversteer or traction rolling on high-bite tracks.
Aim for a neutral balance where the car enters corners predictably without forcing you to fight the rear end.
