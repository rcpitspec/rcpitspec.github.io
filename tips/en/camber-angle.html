RC Camber Angle Setup Guide: Basics & Tuning Tips

Common Symptoms

  • Rear end feels loose or spins out easily when entering or exiting turns
  • Unsure what baseline camber angle to start with
  • Tires wear down prematurely on the inside or outside edge

5 Steps to Try First

  1. Stand the rear tires more upright if the rear is loose … A very common pit mistake is running too much negative camber, which shrinks the tire contact patch on straightaways.
  2. Less camber gives initial bite; more camber supports mid-corner roll … Adjusting angles shifts when tire grip peaks through a turn.
  3. Start around -1.5° front and -1.0° to -1.5° rear … This provides a well-balanced starting point before fine-tuning in 0.5° increments.
  4. Read your tire wear to verify ideal contact … When the tire tread wears evenly across its full width, contact under chassis roll is dialed in.
  5. Measure with battery installed at race ride height … Because suspension geometry changes as the chassis settles, checking angles in race trim is essential.

※ These are general guidelines.
Setup effects can vary depending on chassis and surface grip. It's best to change one setting at a time.

A Closer Look at Camber Setup

Camber Angle and Handling Trends

SettingTire PositionHandling Characteristic (Typical Trend)
More Upright (0° to -1.0°)Perpendicular to the trackMaximum contact in a straight line; quick initial steering and strong forward drive.
More Leaned (-1.5° to -2.5°)Top of tire tilted inwardTire rolls flat onto the surface mid-corner, improving lateral grip deep in turns.

Why Rear Camber Around -1.0° Is a Proven Baseline

When drivers complain of a nervous, loose car at the track, excessive negative rear camber is frequently the cause.
Leaning the rear tires heavily inward lifts the outer tread off the ground, reducing the rubber in contact with the pavement.
Standing the rear tires back toward -1.0° restores full tread contact, immediately improving forward traction and rear stability.

Tuning Front Camber for Steering Feel

Front camber offers an effective way to adjust steering character.
Setting front tires slightly more upright (closer to -1.0°) sharpens initial turn-in response off center.
Adding modest camber (-1.5° to -2.0°) softens that initial jerk and lets the front end bite more progressively through the apex.

Surface Grip and Traction Roll Prevention

On green, dusty tracks, standing the tires slightly more upright helps tires generate heat and mechanical grip.
Conversely, on high-grip black carpet where traction rolling is a risk, leaning the tires inward can bleed off excess cornering bite and keep all four wheels on the ground.

Avoid Going Too Far

Running extreme negative camber (-3.0° or more) prevents the tire from ever making full contact, destroying grip in both braking and cornering.
It also causes aggressive inside edge coning, rapidly ruining costly tires.
Regularly inspect tire wear with your fingertips, and make adjustments in careful 0.5° steps.
