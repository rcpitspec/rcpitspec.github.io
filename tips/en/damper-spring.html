Setup Tips

How to Choose RC Shock Oil and Springs

Common Symptoms

  • Unsure which shock oil viscosity or spring rate to choose
  • The chassis feels floaty and unsettled, or overly stiff and unresponsive
  • Looking to stabilize handling by adjusting front-to-rear balance

5 Steps to Try First

  1. Start with the kit manual baseline … Without a consistent starting point, it is difficult to judge whether changes made the car better or worse.
  2. Separate spring rate (roll amount) from oil (speed) … Avoid changing both simultaneously to keep cause and effect clear.
  3. Stiffen the rear if the tail slides out … Going one step stiffer on rear springs or standing up the rear shocks can add stability.
  4. Soften the front if steering feels twitchy … Allowing the front end to roll slightly more tends to smooth out aggressive turn-in.
  5. Match shock lengths and bleed out air bubbles … Imbalance between left and right shocks is a frequent cause of poor straight-line tracking.

※ These are general guidelines.
Setup effects can vary depending on chassis and surface grip. It's best to change one setting at a time.

A Closer Look at Shocks and Springs

Springs vs. Shock Oil: Understanding the Difference

ComponentPrimary FunctionWhen Stiffened (Typical Trend)
SpringsSupports chassis weight; limits total body rollReduces body roll, but overly stiff springs reduce mechanical grip.
Shock Oil (Damping)Controls the speed of suspension movementSlows down roll and pitch transitions, creating a calmer feel.

Front-to-Rear Balance

Running matched hardness front and rear, or setting the rear slightly stiffer, is widely regarded as a stable foundation.
Stiffening the rear springs or using slightly heavier rear oil often gives the rear tires more support during mid-corner and exit acceleration.
Conversely, on high-grip surfaces where initial steering feels overly aggressive, softening front springs or oil can bleed off excess front grip and make the car easier to drive.

Shock Mounting Angles

Altering the shock angle on the shock tower or suspension arm changes suspension leverage without changing components.
Standing the shocks more upright creates a firmer effective rate, which tends to increase support and forward traction.
Laying the shocks down generally softens initial response and allows the chassis to roll more freely into corners.

Assembly and Maintenance

Even an ideal setup will perform inconsistently if shocks are unevenly built.
Periodically inspect shock rebound and ensure all air bubbles are thoroughly bled from the oil.
Because silicone oil causes standard rubber O-rings to swell over time, refreshing oil and replacing worn seals helps maintain consistent damping.

Avoid Going Too Far

Making suspension too stiff causes the car to bounce across small bumps, losing vital tire contact with the surface.
Conversely, running excessively soft setups can cause chassis bottoming, which frequently triggers sudden spins or rollovers.
When lost, returning to the kit standard baseline and making single-step spring adjustments is a reliable way forward.
