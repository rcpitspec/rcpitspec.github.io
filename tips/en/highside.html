Rollover & High-Grip Setup

How to Stop RC Car Traction Roll (Tipping Over)

Symptoms

  • Inside wheels lift up and the car barrel-rolls into corners
  • Chassis violently digs in on high-traction carpet or asphalt
  • Car flips unexpectedly over curbs or with quick steering inputs

5 Quick Adjustments to Try First

  1. Reduce Steering Dual Rate … Dialing back 5% to 10% on your radio prevents aggressive initial bite
  2. Add Negative Camber Angle … Tipping the tire inward relieves excessive outer-shoulder digging
  3. Lower Overall Ride Height … A lower center of gravity tends to reduce rollover
  4. Lay Down Shock Angles … Shocks that are too upright resist roll too abruptly and trigger flips
  5. Reduce Droop (Limit Uptravel) … Limiting suspension extension prevents inside wheels from jacking the car

※ These are general guidelines.
Setup effects can vary depending on chassis and surface grip. It's best to change one setting at a time.

In-Depth Setup Guide

When the Car Tips vs What to Check

PhaseMost Likely Areas to Check
Initial corner turn-inSteering dual rate, tire shoulder bite, aggressive input
Mid-corner roll-overCamber angle, ride height, heavy body shell (high CG)
Direction change / chicane snapShock angles, spring stiffness, sway bar balance
Hitting curbs or track markersDroop / rebound travel, shock rebound damping, ride height

Steering Rate & Turn-in Smoothness

When track grip skyrockets, reducing your transmitter's steering dual rate is the fastest trackside remedy.
Carving a round, progressive arc into the corner rather than jerking the wheel prevents the front tires from violently snagging the surface.

Camber Angles

On a high-grip surface, adding a little more negative camber often helps the tires slide slightly rather than catching their edges.
As the chassis leans during cornering, a tilted tire rolls onto a smaller outer shoulder area, effectively venting excess bite.

Lowering Center of Gravity

Lowering the ride height brings the center of gravity down, which tends to make the car harder to tip over.
Opting for lightweight bodyshells and mounting them as low as possible also removes top-heavy mass that encourages rollover.

Suspension (Shock Angles & Droop)

Laying down shock angles provides a more progressive damping curve that absorbs side loads smoothly.
Limiting suspension droop (downward travel) is another proven technique that prevents the inside suspension from pushing up and flipping the chassis.

Tire Edges & Sidewall Bite

When the front tires bite too hard, many racers reduce how sharply the outer edge (shoulder) of the tire catches.
Softening that initial edge bite smooths out turn-in and can help prevent sudden tripping.

Watch Out for Over-Adjusting

Over-correcting for traction roll can sap your corner speed and create stubborn understeer or rear-end sliding.
Keep dialing back bite incrementally until the car corners flat without losing necessary agility.
