How to Fix a Loose Rear End & Spinouts

Symptoms

  • Rear end steps out or slides mid-corner
  • Car spins out immediately upon rolling onto the throttle
  • Rear feels constantly nervous and difficult to control

5 Quick Adjustments to Try First

  1. Reduce Rear Negative Camber … Excess camber reduces the contact patch; start around -1.0°
  2. Add or Increase Wing Downforce … Running without a wing leaves the rear light and unstable
  3. Stiffen the Rear Suspension … Stand up rear shocks or try one step stiffer springs/oil
  4. Increase Rear Toe-In … Adds rear stability and traction, though corner speed may slightly drop
  5. Reduce Steering Dual Rate … Dialing back 5% to 10% on your transmitter can instantly calm the car

※ These are general guidelines.
Setup effects can vary depending on chassis and surface grip. It's best to change one setting at a time.

In-Depth Setup Guide

When the Rear Slides vs What to Check

PhaseMost Likely Areas to Check
Corner entry snapRear diff too soft, front grip overpowering rear
Mid-corner drift / looseRear camber, rear suspension stiffness, rear wing
On-throttle exit spinoutRear diff too stiff, aggressive throttle input
Braking instabilityExcessive brake bias or sudden braking (esp. 2WD)

Rear Camber Angle

Running too much negative camber tilts the tire off its flat contact patch, making the rear feel loose and floaty.
Setting rear camber closer to -1.0° maximizes tire contact and usually provides an immediate traction boost.

Wing & Aerodynamics

A higher or larger rear wing exerts more aerodynamic downforce, planting the rear at mid to high speeds.
Mounting the rear of the body shell slightly higher can also help stabilize the rear end on fast circuits.

Rear Suspension (Shocks & Springs)

Standing up the rear shocks or running slightly stiffer springs keeps the rear end from rolling excessively.
Conversely, an overly soft rear suspension can lean past its grip limit, causing sudden snap oversteer.

Rear Toe-In & Ride Height

Adding rear toe-in locks in straight-line and mid-corner stability, though it may slightly reduce rotation and top speed.
Setting rear ride height roughly 0.5mm higher than the front is another quick adjustment to try in the pits.

Differential Tuning

A rear gear diff that is too light can cause corner entry oversteer, while an excessively stiff diff breaks both tires loose on exit.
If overall rear traction is lacking on low-bite tracks, switching to a ball differential can provide smoother traction delivery.

Driving Technique

Smoother, progressive steering inputs keep weight transfer gradual and protect the rear tires from breaking loose.
Be mindful of aggressive curb strikes or cold, freshly cleaned track surfaces where grip takes a few laps to build.

Watch Out for Over-Adjusting

Adding excessive rear traction can cause stubborn understeer or provoke traction rolls on high-grip surfaces.
Find a comfortable baseline where the car feels planted without resisting turn-in.
