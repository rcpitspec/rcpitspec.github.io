Tracking & Alignment Guide

Why Your RC Car Won't Drive Straight & How to Fix It

Symptoms

  • Car constantly drifts left or right on the main straight
  • Steering trim wanders and won't stay centered between runs
  • Chassis wanders, twitches, or darts unpredictably down straights

5 Quick Adjustments to Try First

  1. Inspect Servo Mounting & Saver Slop … A shifting servo or loose saver spring prevents true centering
  2. Set Front Toe Closer to Neutral / Toe-In … Excessive front toe-out causes twitchy, wandering straight tracking
  3. Check for Suspension Binding … Disconnect shocks and ensure arms swing freely under their own weight
  4. Match Left/Right Ride Height & Droop … Unequal heights or travel cause the car to pull under throttle/braking
  5. Avoid Excessive Rear Toe-In … Too much can actually make the car wander on the straight

※ These are general guidelines.
Setup effects can vary depending on chassis and surface grip. It's best to change one setting at a time.

In-Depth Setup Guide

Symptoms vs What to Check

SymptomMost Likely Areas to Check
Trim drifts after every turnLoose servo mounts, worn servo saver, loose tie-rod ball cups
Consistently pulls to one sideUnequal ride height, camber mismatch, tire diameter
Twitching / hunting down straightExcessive front toe-out, rear toe-in set too high
Veers under acceleration/brakingSuspension binding, uneven droop, sway bar imbalance

Steering Centering & Servo Saver Slop

The most common cause of steering drift is a servo assembly that fails to return to exact mechanical zero.
Check for play in the servo saver spring, worn plastic servo horns, or loose servo mounting screws that allow the servo body to shift.
It's best to center the steering mechanically using turnbuckle linkages before relying on transmitter sub-trim.

Toe Angles (Front & Rear)

Running too much front toe-out makes the car hyper-sensitive to every tiny road ripple, causing straightaway hunting.
Resetting front toe to zero (neutral) or adding slight toe-in provides much calmer, locked-in straight-line tracking.
Rear toe-in can also cause wandering when overdone; many racers start around 2.5° to 3°.

Suspension Binding & Free Movement

If one suspension corner binds, the chassis will not settle evenly, pulling the car toward the stiffer side.
Pop off your shock absorbers and lift each suspension arm; check that it falls freely without any resistance or catching.
Bent hinge pins or overtightened pivot balls are frequent culprits behind mysterious steering pulls.

Ride Height & Droop Symmetry

Left-to-right discrepancies in ride height or suspension droop induce chassis tweak during acceleration and braking.
Place the car on a flat setup board and confirm that ride height and downstop gaps match perfectly on both sides.

Sway Bar Balance

If an anti-roll bar engages earlier on one side than the other, it creates an asymmetric chassis twist on power.
Adjust the drop-link turnbuckles until the sway bar lifts simultaneously on both left and right wheels.

Watch Out for Over-Adjusting

Setting your car up exclusively for bullet-straight tracking (heavy toe-in, super stiff rear) can deaden corner turn-in.
Aim for a balanced geometry that tracks hands-off down the straightaway while still carving smoothly into turns.
